# The Chronicle of Bedegraine

**The Battle of Bedegraine — Indecisive.** The conroi: Sir Erik the Cruel, Dame Cwyllog, Sir Emrys.

*This record picks up on the first day of the battle, at the third turn of the fighting.*

## Day 1, Turn 3: The Pictish Pikemen

Painted mountain men came down on the conroi, bare of armour and carrying long spears. Such men were known to flee once a third of their number lay dead. Fortune left the knights free to choose their fate, and they let the encounter stand. All three rode in valorously. Six pikemen came on, and they split two to each knight. One of those facing Dame Cwyllog was a man of middling build with an old sword-scar down his jaw. Sir Erik the Cruel held the flank, but his fighting there went unrecorded. Dame Cwyllog struck the telling blow of the fight.

**Deeds of note**
- ☠ Dame Cwyllog cut down Pictish Pikeman 2, who fell with a Major Wound. +10 Glory

## Day 1, Turn 4: Rest

The conroi drew back to the rear to rest and recover their spirits.

## Day 1, Turn 5: The Pictish Javeliniers

The conroi returned to the field and found Pictish Knifemen before them. Once more fortune left the choice to the knights, and Dame Cwyllog chose instead to ride at the Javeliniers, skirmishers who hurl javelins and then melt away. The commander agreed. Sir Erik went on foot and made no charge. He held the flank, and his deeds went unrecorded. Dame Cwyllog and Sir Emrys rode recklessly. One volley met them before they reached the skirmishers. Nine foes took the field, three to each knight, and Dame Cwyllog did the killing.

**Deeds of note**
- 🐎 Javelins from Pictish Javeliniers 2 and 8 struck Dame Cwyllog's horse.
- 🐎 A javelin from Pictish Javeliniers 9 struck Sir Emrys's horse.
- Pictish Javeliniers 5 and 6 both fumbled their throws and missed.
- In one round did Dame Cwyllog behead 2 Picts and grievously wound another.
- ⚔ ☠ Dame Cwyllog struck Pictish Javeliniers 8 a critical blow, knocking him down and killing him. +5 Glory
- ⚔ ☠ Dame Cwyllog struck Pictish Javeliniers 5 a critical blow, knocking him down and killing him. +5 Glory
- ☠ Dame Cwyllog felled Pictish Javeliniers 2 with a Major Wound. +5 Glory
- Sir Emrys fumbled against Pictish Javeliniers 6, and both missed. Sir Emrys's weapon was dropped or broken.

## Day 2, Turn 1: The Cambrian Archers

The first day ended. When the second dawned, the conroi mustered under Sir Emrys's leadership, its spirits whole again through homage to Arthur. King Arthur held the ridge, and the day opened with an exchange of arrows across it. Archers from the hills, carrying the dreaded longbow, loosed on the line, men known to flee if a third of them were hurt. Dame Cwyllog and Sir Emrys rode recklessly into one flight of arrows. The shafts struck both their mounts, and both knights were forced to dismount and fight on foot. Six archers came against them, three to each knight, and drew daggers. A grievous hurt was done to Dame Cwyllog in the press.

**Deeds of note**
- 🐎 An arrow from Cambrian Archer 6 struck Sir Emrys's horse.
- ⚔ 🐎 Cambrian Archer 1 put a critical shaft into Dame Cwyllog's horse.
- Sir Emrys fumbled against Cambrian Archer 6 and lost his weapon, dropped or broken.
- ☠ Dame Cwyllog felled Cambrian Archer 5 with a Major Wound. +25 Glory
- ⚔ ⚠ Cambrian Archer 1 caught Dame Cwyllog with a critical stroke of his dagger, for 29. The wound was grievous.

## Day 2, Turn 2: The Knights of Lothian

Then Lot's horsemen came up the hill. An unusually large conroi of his Lothian knights, more of them than usual and harder-hitting than most, thundered at the ridge, and word ran along the line that King Arthur was wounded but would not withdraw. Dame Cwyllog and Sir Emrys, both still on foot, stood prudently to receive them. The Lothians countercharged, three of them, one or two to each knight, and the conroi braced as the charge broke upon it.

**Deeds of note**
- ⚔ Sir Emrys met Knight of Lothian 3's charge with a critical stroke. The Lothian's weapon shattered on the charge.

## Day 2, Turn 3: The Irish Kerns

Along the ridge, Count Sanam gave ground at the first push and left Lot's right flank bare. Next came Irish kerns, poorly armed and unarmoured men brought across the Irish Sea to fight for pay. They had little discipline, but there were a great many of them. Dame Cwyllog and Sir Emrys rode recklessly into eight of them, four to each knight, and the kerns took up spears. A lean, grey-eyed kern carrying an old, long-healed slave-brand struck Sir Emrys a terrible blow, and Sir Emrys went down. With him fallen, the same kern turned on Dame Cwyllog. She left three of the kerns on the ground.

**Deeds of note**
- ⚔ ⚠ Irish Kern 8 struck Sir Emrys a critical stroke with his spear, for 29. The wound was grievous.
- ☠ Dame Cwyllog felled Irish Kern 1 with a Major Wound. +10 Glory
- ⚠ Sir Emrys was stuck a mortal spear wound and knocked form his horse, down and near death.
- ☠ Dame Cwyllog felled Irish Kern 7 with a Major Wound. +10 Glory
- ⚔ ☠ Dame Cwyllog struck Irish Kern 3 a critical blow and left him unconscious. +10 Glory
- Dame Cwyllog did cry to her Pagan Gods to set fear into the Kerns who fled for their lives.  Sir Emrys was dragged from the battle field.

## Day 2, Turns 4–5: Rest

The conroi retired to the rear and stayed there two turns, and its spirits rose nearly to full. From the rear they heard the shout go up that Ban and Leodegrance had come, and saw fresh knights burst from the woods. Lot fell back behind his shield wall in good order, and there was no pursuit.

## The Close of the Field

The second day ended, and so did the battle. Neither host gave way, and Bedegraine was judged **indecisive**.
