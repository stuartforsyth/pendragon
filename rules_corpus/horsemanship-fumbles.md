In the Pendragon RPG, fumbling a **Horsemanship roll** (rolling a natural 20, unless your skill is 20 or higher) is always highly dramatic, but the specific consequences depend entirely on the situation you are in:

### **1. General Riding & Inquiry Fumbles**

- **General Riding (Deed):** If you are attempting a difficult riding feat (such as jumping a hedge or racing) and fumble, you fail to execute the action. You do, however, gain an **experience check** to Horsemanship because we learn from our greatest mistakes. The Gamemaster may also apply a negative modifier to any follow-up actions.
- **Knowledge/Equine Appraisal (Inquiry):** If you roll Horsemanship to identify a horse's breed or appraise its value and fumble, you fail to retrieve the correct information, but still gain an experience check.

### **2. Controlling Mounts in Combat**

- **Non-Combat Trained Horses:** If you ride a normal riding horse (like a rouncy or palfrey) into battle, you must make a **Control Mount** roll at the start of each round at a -5 penalty. Fumbling this roll means **your knight is instantly thrown** and must immediately make an Emergency Dismount roll.
- **Wounded Horses:** If your horse takes 5 or more points of damage in combat, you must make a Horsemanship roll at the start of the next round with a penalty equal to the horse's lost Hit Points. Fumbling this means you are thrown.

### **3. Emergency Dismounts & Falling**

- **Emergency Dismount:** When your horse falls or you are about to be thrown, you roll Horsemanship to land safely. Fumbling this roll is catastrophic: **you take 2D6 falling damage** (dropping all held weapons), and if the horse itself has fallen, **it lands on top of you**, inflicting the horse's full normal Damage value (though armor protects against this latter damage normally).
- **Reducing Falling Damage:** Usually, when falling from a horse, a successful Horsemanship roll reduces your falling damage by 1D6 (2D6 on a critical). However, **fumbling this roll doubles the number of falling damage dice** you take.

### **4. Mounted Lance Charges**

- If you fumble a Mounted Charge with a couched spear or lance, your weapon breaks and you must roll Horsemanship to determine the disaster. Fumbling that follow-up Horsemanship roll means **your lance tip buries itself in the earth, bringing down both you and your horse; your lance is shattered, and you take 3D6 damage** which armor does not protect against.

### **5. Horse Care**

- If you are tending to a sick, injured, or overworked horse and fumble your Horsemanship roll, the animal becomes **terribly weak or sore**. For the long term, your horse suffers a **-2 penalty to its Horse Survival Roll** in the next Winter Phase, and you suffer a **-10 penalty to all Horsemanship rolls** until a farrier can tend to the mount.

### **6. Hunting & Obstacles**

- **Jumping Streams/Ditches:** Fumbling a Horsemanship roll to jump a small stream or ditch during a hunt causes **both you and your horse to fall, taking 1D6 damage**.
- **Squire Exercises:** If you are a young squire practicing turning after a charge and fumble, the **horse rears**. You must succeed at an unopposed DEX roll to stay in the saddle; failing means you fall for 1D6 damage and face the merciless laughter of your peers.
