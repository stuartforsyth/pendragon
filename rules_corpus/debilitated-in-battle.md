**A Debilitated knight can still fight a Battle, but every round is a bet against Aggravation, and Battle's own procedure gives them one honourable way out — Retire to the Rear without a Cowardly check.** Debilitated is checked the instant a knight drops below their Unconscious Characteristic, takes a Major Wound, survives a Mortal Wound, fumbles First Aid, or contracts illness/poison, and it stays checked until a successful `Chirurgery` roll **and** recovery to half Total HP (core rules, ch. 11 — Injury & Health, pp. 189–192) — neither of which happens mid-Battle Turn.

## 1 · Getting there: Major Wound and Unconscious in a Battle Turn

Fighting an Encounter is ordinary melee (see [[fighting-the-battle]]); the same wound rules apply. A single blow at or above the knight's CON is a **Major Wound** — they go unconscious immediately, check `Debilitated`, and roll once on Table 11.1: Characteristic Lost. They may attempt an **Emergency Dismount** first if mounted. Dropping to HP at or below their Unconscious Characteristic through accumulated Minor Wounds has the same effect, without the Characteristic roll (core rules, ch. 11, pp. 189–192).

An **Unconscious** knight left on the field may be taken prisoner — Capture is handled in the Squire Skill step of the Battle Turn, or by an opponent withholding their blow (see [[fighting-the-battle]]).

## 2 · Fighting on while Debilitated

Nothing in the Battle Turn procedure stops a Debilitated-but-conscious knight from choosing a Posture and fighting the next Encounter — Debilitated is a Health state, not a battlefield status. But two core-rules costs follow them into every round they stay in:

> [!important] Strenuous activity while Debilitated makes Aggravation certain
> Table 11.2 rates **fighting** as Strenuous activity. A **Healthy** knight only risks Aggravation there if carrying many small wounds or below half HP; a **Debilitated** knight's Aggravation is **certain** — one or more Hit Points lost, with no wound recorded, on top of whatever the Encounter itself does, for every round they keep fighting.

- **Table 11.2 — Activity and Aggravation**

  | Activity | Examples | Healthy | Debilitated |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | No activity | rest, eat sensibly, sleep | None (best recovery) | None (best recovery) |
  | Light | short walks, conversation, short letters | None | None unless overdone |
  | Moderate | longer walks, riding (even briefly), dancing, romance, arguing | None | Possible (GM option) |
  | Strenuous | fighting, running, climbing, significant travel | Possible if many small wounds or below half HP | **Certain** |

- **A knight who is actually Unconscious — HP at or below their Unconscious Characteristic, or the instant after a Major Wound — is normally out of the fight completely: no acting, no rolls, nothing.** *Fighting beyond debilitation* is the one exception that lets them act anyway, and it works round by round, not once for the whole Encounter:
  - **Each round they want to act, roll twice: an unopposed `Energetic` roll, and a separate roll against current HP (roll equal to or under current HP to succeed).** Both must succeed. Fail either, and that round they're out — no action, same as ordinary Unconscious.
  - **Pass both, and they act normally that round** — but it costs **1 Aggravation** (1+ HP lost, no wound recorded) regardless of how the round itself goes.
  - **Instead of acting normally, they may choose to act with slow deliberation:** a **−10 penalty to all Skills** that round, in exchange for a **reduced** Aggravation cost (the book doesn't fix a number below 1 — GM's call on the spot) (core rules, ch. 11, p. 192).
- **First Aid does not clear Debilitated** from a Major or Mortal Wound, so a squire's battlefield First Aid (Squire Skill roll, see [[fighting-the-battle]]) stops one wound bleeding but does nothing about the Aggravation risk above.

## 3 · Retiring to the Rear

Ordinary Retire to the Rear is available to the whole conroi once Morale hits 0 or falls below an Encounter's minimum (see [[battlefield-position-and-surrender]]) — a Debilitated knight retires with everyone else, no separate roll required, and Attendants may treat wounds with a random First Aid 14+1D6 per knight once out.

Debilitated has one further, narrower effect of its own: a knight left **Alone on the Field** (deserted, Passion-Mad, or unhorsed with a failed Squire remount roll) who is `Valorous` 16+ would normally have to check `Cowardly` to retire instead of fighting on — **being Debilitated waives that check.** Leaving the field while too hurt to fight is not treated as cowardice by the rules as written (GM Book, ch. 6 — Battle, pp. 131–132).

## 4 · Across a multi-day Battle

A single-day Battle ends before Deterioration matters, but a Battle that runs across several days (see [[running-a-battle]]) exposes a still-Debilitated knight to the ordinary weekly clock, scaled to the gap between sessions: without a successful `Chirurgery` roll, **Deterioration** deals 1D6 damage with no wound recorded — the only healing-side consequence that applies to a knight who is still Debilitated when the next day's fighting resumes (core rules, ch. 11, pp. 192–194; see [[aggravation-and-deterioration]]). Natural Healing Rate resumes only once Chirurgery has actually restored the knight to half Total HP and cleared Debilitated (core rules, ch. 11, pp. 189–192, 198–199; see [[healing-recovery]]).

*Core rules, ch. 11 — Injury & Health, pp. 189–194, 198–199. GM Book, ch. 6 — Battle, pp. 125–132.*
