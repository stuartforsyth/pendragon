Horses are damaged like characters — their own `SIZ`/`CON`/Hit Points, wounds recorded normally. Three things are specific to them: a **5-point threshold** that degrades the rider's control, the **horse's `SIZ`** as the knockdown trigger for *both* animal and rider, and the fact that **only a character with `Horsemanship 15+`** may treat one.

## Getting the horse hit

- **Attacking a horse deliberately** is dishonorable between knights: **−1 Honor**. A **partial success** by the rider lets the horse benefit from his **Shield/Parry Protection**. Attacking from foot negates the attacker's −5 Height penalty, but the mounted opponent still gets their **+5**. *(ch. 7, pp. 137–138)*
- **Missiles** do not discriminate: each round of barrage, roll **1D6 — 1–4 hits the mount, 5–6 the rider**. *(GM Book, ch. 6 — Battle, pp. 129–130)*
- **Horse armor** is **not issued automatically**, even to household knights, and the horse needs the **STR** to wear it. *(Table 10.7, p. 187)*

| Type | Armor | Horse STR needed |
|---|---:|---|
| Caparison (open / half / full) | 0 | Any — decorative |
| Padding, Full | 1 | 30 (Small Charger and greater) |
| Gambeson, Half | 2 | 32 (Charger and greater) |
| Gambeson, Full | 4 | 34 (Large Charger and greater) |

## The 5-point threshold

**When any horse takes 5+ points of damage in a round**, check injury effects — and thereafter **roll `Horsemanship` every round before acting, at a penalty equal to the horse's total lost Hit Points**. A combat-trained horse settles on a critical *or* an ordinary success. *(CRB, pp. 181)*  A failed role means the horse will not settle and the entire round is spent trying to get the horse under control.  Once settled a horse won't need another check unless wounded again.

The injury check is **Table 10.6**, rolled on `Horsemanship` *(p. 187)* if the GM feels the horse needs immediate and extra care (crb187):

| Result | Immediate | Long-term |
|---|---|---|
| **Critical** | Energized — may move fast for **double** the normal duration | No effect |
| **Success** | Carries on as normal | No effect |
| **Failure** | Weak/sore: **−5** to all `Horsemanship` rolls until a farrier tends it | **−1** to the next Winter Phase Horse Survival Roll |
| **Fumble** | Terribly weak/sore: **−10** until a farrier tends it | **−2** to that roll |

## Going down — the horse's SIZ is the trigger

**If total damage to *either* horse or rider exceeds the horse's `SIZ`**, the **horse** makes a `DEX` roll to stay standing; failure knocks it down. *(ch. 7, p. 150; and p. 134)*

The rider then attempts an **Emergency Dismount** — unopposed `Horsemanship`, **Table 7.4**:

- **Critical:** lands on his feet, upright next round, no damage, **keeps all weapons**.
- **Success:** lands on his feet, no damage, but **drops** held weapons.
- **Failure:** improper dismount — **1D6** damage, drops all weapons.
- **Fumble:** **2D6** damage, drops all weapons; **if the horse fell, it lands on him for the horse's Damage**.

If the Emergency Dismount fails outright the rider is **crushed by the falling steed for the horse's normal Damage** — armor protects normally. *(ch. 7, pp. 150–151)*

**Escaping from under a fallen horse:** each round, roll the rider's **`STR` vs the horse's `SIZ`**; each helper adds **+5** to the strongest person's `STR`, who makes the roll.

> Distinguish this from an ordinary fall from the saddle, which is only **1D6**.

## Treating and replacing it

- ==**Only a character with `Horsemanship 15+` may apply `First Aid` to a wounded steed.**== Horse **Healing Rate = CON/5**, one roll per wound, as for humans. *(p. 187)*
- **In battle:** a successful **`Squire` roll** brings a fresh horse mid-fight, and **Retiring to the Rear** allows lost horses to be replaced on **`Squire Skill` or `Horsemanship`**. ==Being unhorsed **and** failing the Squire roll for a remount leaves the knight **Alone on the Field**== — his own commander at **Battle −10**, still draining the conroi's Morale. *(GM Book, ch. 6, pp. 130–132)*
- **At year's end:** every charger, destrier, or unique horse rolls **1D20 on Table 14.7, the Horse Survival Roll** — where a failed or fumbled Table 10.6 check returns as a **−1 or −2**. *(ch. 14, p. 232)*

## Adjacent rules that bite in the same moment

- **Mounted Charge fumble** with a couched lance sends the rider to **Table 7.2**: a failure buries the tip and launches him from the saddle for **2D6**; a fumble brings **both him and the horse** down for **3D6**. Armor protects against neither. *(p. 141)*
- **Trample** works only on **prone** opponents and uses the horse's **normal (non-charging) Damage**. *(pp. 142–143)*
- **Too Big for Your Horse** — if the rider's `SIZ`×2 (−5 if unarmored) exceeds the horse's `STR`: **−5** `Horsemanship`, **−5** horse Movement Rate, counts as Forced March on long travel, and horse Damage **−1D6**. *(ch. 7, p. 151)*
