Tournaments — originally **hastiludes** ("spear play") — are sporting events where knights with blunted weapons try to unhorse each other. Events use **"arms of peace"**: rebated weapons and lances built to break easily.

**Jousts are open only to tried warriors** who know how to ride armored and how to fall when hit. A competition may be restricted to nobles of rank, to knights who can prove **three generations of knightly ancestors**, or to those who can afford a steep entry fee. Prizes and Glory await the victors; **humiliation and the loss of a prized horse or armor** await the losers.

Announced **months in advance**; knights arrive a week to a day early, lodging in the **two villages that bound the melee field** — ==which village you lodge in determines which melee team you are on== — or in pavilions near the grounds. A **bohort** may be held where squires practise and show off against their peers.

## Entering

- **Only knights may enter.** Vassal, household or mercenary makes no difference. **Squires and cavalry** may take part only in their own special events.
- **Knights without honor or of ill repute may not participate.**
- ==**Participation in disguise is allowed.**==
- **A fee is required.**

## Short-Form Tournaments

For a tourney with no unusual events planned — a handful of rolls, fast enough to resolve in passing while the knights are on adventure.

**1 · Tournament Difficulty Level (TDL).** ==Equal to the **worst Charge Skill among the Player-knights**==, then modified by Table 3.14.

**2 · Size.** Roll on **Table 3.12: Master Tournament Table**, which also fixes Participant and Champion's Glory and the prize. **Add +5** in the Tournament or Grail Quest Periods, **+10** in the Twilight Period.

**3 · Resolve.** Make an **opposed roll against the TDL** — a **Weapon Skill** of choice for the Melee, **Charge** for the Joust — on Table 3.15. Then make a **second opposed roll on the same table for accumulated damage**. ==It is therefore possible to **win the event and still be grievously wounded**.== A knight who competes in one event and stays healthy enough for the other may do both, **doubling the potential Glory**.

**Courtly Rolls** (Table 3.12) are rolled **even under the short form**.

- **Barber's Skill** — unless seen to by a wife, lover, or their own entourage, seriously wounded knights are attended by a professional with this value in **both First Aid and Chirurgery**.

|---|---|---|

==**Damage here is unaffected by armor**== — it represents small hurts accumulated through the tourney that penetrated the harness, and **may only be healed by Natural Healing**.

## The shape of the event

**The Invocation.** On the morning, the sponsor gives a speech; contestants parade before judges and onlookers. Heralds present the **Lady of the Tournament** — the sponsor's wife, or an available lady whose hand may be offered to the victor. The sponsor introduces the **Grand Marshall**, the **judges** (well-known veterans and important ladies) and the **heralds**, displays the prizes, and **states the terms of capture and the ransoms** to be enforced in the melee.

**Each day** ends with the judges naming the best fighter and awarding a prize, then a **feast** with music, dances, jongleurs, bards and troubadours late into the night.

**At the close**, event winners are named and given prizes, and the champion — the **knight of honor** (*chevalier d'honneur*) — is acclaimed. All combatants meet unarmed and unarmored in the lists to embrace and **prove no grudges are held**. Captives arrange to ransom back armor, horses, or their own bodies. A final feast is held; those too wounded to travel **remain as guests of their host**.
