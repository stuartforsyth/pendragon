## The Grand Melee

A **mock battle** over a field of several square miles with vague boundaries, between the two villages where the teams are lodged. At either end a **recet** is marked out — the team's rear area, banked and guarded by armed footmen who keep opposing knights out.

A melee may last **half a day** for a neighborhood tournament, or **several days** for regional and royal ones, with contestants dwindling as they are captured or wounded.

Contestants divide into two even sides, **charge with lances at the signal**, and fall into melee once the lances break. Teams are divided into **conroi**-like groups that support each other and **share loot and ransom costs**. ==A captive may be **rescued by friends before he is brought into the recet**.== In the Boy King Period knights are aided by **squires and kippers**, whose job is to lead or drag captives off the field into the recet — a practice that **ends during the Conquest Period**. During the day knights may **retire to their recet** for first aid, rest, a drink or a snack, but will rush back if their side is losing.

### Procedure

==**A tournament melee is run with the full rules of Chapter 6: Battle**, because that is exactly what it is.== Player-knights choose a team and a **conroi leader** before it begins.

- The GM assembles **Encounters and Opportunities appropriate to a tournament** — knights exclusively.
- **Melee size corresponds to regular battle sizes, but ==Intensity is only half==.**
- Participation Glory comes from **Table 6.6: Battle Participation Glory**, by melee size.
- Individual Glory for defeating opponents is awarded as always — but ==**with rebated weapons, award only one-tenth the normal Glory**==.

**The Grand Prize** goes to whoever the judges — tracking deeds of prowess through the heralds — decide was the most valiant, dashing and effective.

### The rules of the melee, in full

- **Lances and sharp weapons are rebated.**
- **A knight is defeated when unhorsed, knocked unconscious, killed (accidentally, of course), or on surrendering.**
- When an opponent is defeated, ==**their steed may be taken**== and becomes the winner's prize **if the knight or his squire can bring it to the safe area and leave it there**.
- Knights on the same team, and especially the same conroi, **are expected to assist each other**.
- **Multiple opponents may attack a single knight.**

## The Joust

Formal jousting events take about **ten years into Arthur's reign** to appear, growing from ostentatious challenges by prideful show-offs into formalized elimination contests. They are often placed **before** the melee — still the main event — so that as many knights as possible can take part, since the melee often removes a contestant entirely. Each contestant **draws a random opponent** and meets him for **up to three passes** on a ground called the **list**.

### Procedure

A series of **one-on-one contests using opposed Charge Skills**; each roll is a **pass**. The intention is **not to kill** but to unhorse the opponent humiliatingly, or failing that to be **first to break three lances** on his shield. Jousting lances are specially built to break more often.

==**Tournament jousting uses Table 3.16, not the normal Mounted Charge results**== — the Charge Skill is still used, but the outcomes differ. **Unrebated** joust events, held by bloodthirsty knights, use the **normal Mounted Charge procedure** instead.

### Table 3.16 — Joust Results (opposed Charge)

| Opposed Charge roll | Effect |
|---|---|
| **Critical** | **Perfect hit.** Automatically knocks the opponent from the saddle (**no DEX roll**) for **2D6** falling damage; on a failed **Horsemanship** roll he is **crushed under his horse** (takes the horse's Charge Damage). Lance breaks. |
| **Win** | Hit and break lance; opponent makes a **DEX** roll to remain seated. |
| **Tie, double critical** | Lance to lance, **both lances break**; both make **Horsemanship** rolls to prevent horse knockdown. |
| **Tie, double success** | Both hit, both lances break, both make **DEX** to stay seated. |
| **Tie, double failure** | Both miss, no lances break. |
| **Tie, double fumble** | **1D6:** 1–2 both strike the opponent's horse (**both disqualified**) · 3–4 both miss and the **horses collide** if there is no tilt, each taking the other's full **Charge Damage** (with a tilt, treat as 5–6) · 5–6 both miss but **fall from their horses untouched** for **1D6** each. |
| **Partial success** | Hit; **lance breaks if the Charge roll was an odd number**. |
| **Loss** | Miss; **DEX** roll to remain seated or take **1D6** falling damage. |
| **Fumble** | **1D20:** 1–5 hit the tilt (no tilt → treat as 6–10) · 6–10 **hit the opponent's horse — disqualified** · 11–12 **dropped the lance** before striking, looking like an incompetent youth (**−5 to Courtly Skills for the rest of the tournament**) · 13–14 thrown, horse runs off, **1D6** · 15–16 hit his saddle, possibly angering him (**Forgiving** roll) · 17–18 fall off, **1D6** · 19–20 ==fall with a **foot caught in the stirrup and are dragged for 3D6, no armor protection**==. |

### Joust Glory

- **Winning a round: one-tenth the opponent's Combat Glory.**
- **With unrebated lances: half Combat Glory.**

### Joust scoring *(introduced in the Tournament Period)*

A formal points system for strikes to the chest, helmet or shield. **Three passes** is the commonest encounter; the knight with the most points wins and advances. Some tournaments skip elimination entirely and take the **highest score at the end**.

- **Unhorsing:** 3 points, **or immediate victory**, depending on the rules in force
- **Lance to lance:** 2 points
- **Broken lance:** 1 point
- **Hit but lance not broken:** 0 points, but used as the **tie-breaker**

## Later-Period customs

- A **tilt** — a barrier down the centre of the list — is raised in the Tournament Period to stop the horses colliding, with new lances and armor for safety.
- **Display of the helms:** the day before, all knights lay out helmets and shields in a row to be identified. Judges and ladies scrutinize them and **point out anyone dishonorable, in exile, or otherwise unfit**; if the accusations prove true, ==**the contestant is ejected from the competition**==.
- A **Vespers Tourney** on the evening before the main event lets new knights compete.
- By mid-Tournament Period **the melee is dropped entirely**; individual talent and pomp outweigh martial prowess, entry fees rise steeply, and participants must prove **three generations of knighthood**.
