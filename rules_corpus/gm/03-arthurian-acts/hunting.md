The **Hunting** Skill covers everything in the noble chase except Weapon Skills: what the blasts of the horn mean; whether a beast is one of **chase, venery, or vermin** (field — buck, doe, fox, marten, roe; forest — hart, hind, hare, boar, wolf; lesser — badger, wild cat, otter); care of hounds and their cries; identifying spoor and tracking from it; the best way to kill each animal; and **breaking** — butchering and field-dressing so that everyone from hounds and dog boys to the sponsor gets their correct share.

A hunt **requires dogs and horses**, and is enhanced by **bush beaters** and a **forester** as guide. The rules below assume all of these.

**Armor.** Knights hunt in a **gambeson (4 points)** or **aketon (2 points)**, not full harness. ==Knights in **mail or plate** suffer **−5 to Hunting**, and impose the same −5 on everyone else in their party== — the clanking is audible for a long way.

**Avoidance** is the animal's Statistic for running, hiding and deceiving pursuers. Values are in Chapter 8: Bestiary, p. 173.

## Sequence of a hunt

**1 · The Harboring.** The party selects ("harbors") the quarry. Professional huntsmen gather **tokens** from various beasts — tufts of fur, spoor, pieces of antler, dragon scales — and present them at the **Gathering**, a combined social event and planning session over breakfast in the field. ==The **host**, or the guest with the **most Glory**, chooses which quarry to hunt.==

**Hunting Rounds last about an hour.** A day allows **6 Rounds in winter, 8 in spring and autumn, 10 in summer.** When they are used up, the hunt ends and everyone returns to their lodgings.

The party chooses a **leader** — the character with the highest Hunting Skill. They may **split into several parties** to get more Hunting rolls.

**2 · The Unharboring — setting the track.** Draw **four parallel lines numbered 1–4**, and place the quarry's marker **ahead of position #1**. Every participant gets a marker; leaders and followers are set by their relative positions.

Placement is by **Hunting Skill** and **Movement Rate**:

- Every leader makes an **unopposed Hunting roll**: **critical → #1**, **success → #2**, **failure → #3**, **fumble → #4**. Followers are placed with their leader automatically.
- Then each participant rolls against **their horse's Movement Rate**: **critical** moves the marker **forward one**, **success** stays put, **failure** moves **back one**, **fumble** moves **back two**.

**3 · The Pursuit.** Repeat each Hunting Round until the prey is found (the Abay) or the day ends. ==A marker that ever drops **behind position #4** has fallen too far behind and may not take further part in the hunt.==

Each leader makes an **opposed roll of Hunting against the prey's Avoidance** on Table 3.8. Obstacles are resolved individually by each character, and **must be navigated before advancing**.

### Table 3.8 — Hunting vs Avoidance Results

| Hunter's result | Effect |
|---|---|
| **Critical success** | Encounter an obstacle and **advance two positions** |
| **Win** | Encounter an obstacle and **advance one position** |
| **Partial success or tie** | Encounter an obstacle |
| **Lose** | Move back one position |
| **Fumble** | Move back two positions |

### Table 3.9 — Hunting Obstacles (2D6)

**Only one roll on this table is made per hunting party**, but **each knight tests the indicated Statistic individually**. A failed roll costs **one position from the indicated advance** — possibly separating you from the party, unless everyone slows to let you catch up.

| 2D6 | Obstacle |
|---:|---|
| **2** | Hidden ditch or sudden precipice. Roll the **horse's DEX** to avoid a fall doing **2D6 to both horse and rider**. |
| **3** | Dangerous branches or outcropping rocks. **Horse's DEX** to avoid a fall and **1D6**. |
| **4–5** | A small stream or ditch. **Horsemanship** to jump it. Failure = the horse refused; **fumble** = rider and horse both fall for **1D6**. |
| **6** | Birds flush underfoot. **Horsemanship** to avoid a fall for **1D6**. |
| **7** | Confusing tracks. **Hunting** roll, or lose the prey and **move back one position**. |
| **8** | A fallen tree leaves only a small passage. **Hunting** to find a way through, or **back one position**. |
| **9** | Thorny bramble bushes. The **horse** must make a **CON** roll or take **1D6**. |
| **10** | Hunting horns or barking dogs in the distance. **Awareness** to follow the sound — success ==**moves you to the quarry's position**==. |
| **11** | A dead end. **Awareness**. On a failure, roll **twice more** on this table (ignoring further 11s) and apply both. |
| **12** | ==**The prey comes charging back!** Advance to the Abay.== |

**4 · The Abay.** Once one or more knights catch up with the prey's token, the hunters must drive the animal until it can go no further and turns at bay. Horns sound to call in lost dogs, reckless gallopers and winded hangers-on, all converging for the kill.

- The **first character on the scene** — or the one with the **highest Glory** — may make **one free attack**; alternatively they may hold near the creature until reinforcements arrive and fight it in normal melee.
- ==**Tradition reserves the first blow for the host or honored guest.**== Anyone unwilling to wait rolls **Modest/Proud**; if **Modest succeeds and they strike anyway, they lose 1 Honor**.
- If several want the first attack, the **highest Movement Rate** takes it (ties broken by **DEX**).
- The first attack is an **opposed roll: the hunter's Weapon Skill against the prey's Avoidance**, on Table 3.10.

### Table 3.10 — Weapon vs Avoidance Results

| Weapon vs Avoidance | Effect |
|---|---|
| **Critical success** | Inflict normal Damage **+4D6**. The prey turns angrily and **begins fighting next Round**, if still conscious. |
| **Win** | Inflict normal damage. The prey fights next Round **unless badly hurt**, in which case it Avoids again. |
| **Partial success or tie** | The prey escapes; everyone at the Abay returns to **position #4**. Roll Hunting next segment at **+5**. |
| **Lose** | The prey escapes; everyone at the Abay returns to **position #4**. |
| **Fumble** | The prey escapes; everyone returns to **#4**, and the Player-knight **falls for 1D6** trying to reach it. |

**5 · The Rush.** If the quarry lives after the first attack, **as many as three characters with melee weapons** may attack while the beast tries to escape or to close.

- **If it flees:** it still uses its **Avoidance** on Table 3.10 to oppose these attacks rather than a Combat Skill. With several attackers it takes penalties to Avoidance exactly as it would to a Combat Skill against multiple opponents.
- **If it fights:** use its **Combat Skills** as normal.

**6 · Normal Combat.** If the creature survives the Rush, the hunt becomes ordinary melee. If instead it flees, ==the hunt resumes and returns to the Pursuit.==
