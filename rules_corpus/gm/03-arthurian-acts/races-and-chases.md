Races and chases run on **the same positional track as the hunt**. Draw a series of parallel lines, or mark areas on a map, and place tokens for each party. ==Use **no fewer than four lines**.==

- **In a race**, everyone starts on the same line.
- **In a chase**, the pursued may have a **head start of one or two lines**, depending on the circumstances that began it.

## The Chase Round

Everyone makes an **unopposed roll against their Movement Rate** — or their **steed's**, if mounted:

| Result | Effect |
|---|---|
| **Critical** | Advance **two lines** |
| **Success** | Advance **one line** |
| **Failure** | No advancement |
| **Fumble** | Roll **DEX** (or **Horsemanship** if mounted) — see below |

**On a fumble:** a **failed** DEX/Horsemanship roll means the character has ==**come up lame or winded and may not continue**== (with appropriate consequences if being chased); a **successful** roll means an obstacle merely delays them — roll on **Table 3.9: Hunting Obstacles** if appropriate, or simply have them **miss the next round of rolls**.

**Allied characters on the same line** are treated as **one group**, for simplicity.

## When pursuer and pursued meet

Each time a Chase Round ends with **a pursuer and the pursued on the same line**, they make an **opposed roll based on the Player-knight's stated intention**:

- **Standing and fighting:** Weapon Skill vs Weapon Skill.
- **Losing the trail** in an overland chase by cutting through rough terrain: **opposed Hunting** rolls.
- **Simply speeding away:** treat it as an **Evade** action — the character's **Movement Rate against the pursuer's Weapon Skill**.

**If the pursued wins:** they inflict damage if using a Weapon Skill, and **advance one line**. **If the pursuer wins:** they may inflict damage and have ==**cornered their quarry**, who must either fight a full combat or surrender==.

**Pursuers can catch up more than once** over a chase.

## Ending it

- **A race** is won by the first to reach the final line — but **resolve everyone's Movement Rate rolls before declaring a winner**. On a tie, everyone at the finish makes **opposed DEX (or Horsemanship)** rolls to see who crossed first.
- **A chase** ends when the pursued **reaches the final line and escapes** — getting away from any pursuers who reach it too.
