Any court not of your liege. A common event — escorting a travelling lord or lady, or riding on adventure. **Certain protocols are followed**, and both parties expect formal behaviour.

## 1 · Meeting a patrol

Meeting a border patrol will **certainly** happen shortly after crossing into another county or kingdom. **Courtesy** and **Recognize** carry the encounter:

- **Courtesy** success — a good impression, and relations are smoothed.
- **Recognize** success — learn something about the foreign knights: **their famous Skills and Combat Skills, and which lord they serve**, which is valuable information and opens the social convention of **flattery**.

**If expected:** the knights are welcomed and escorted to the lord's castle, a messenger riding ahead so the hall can be prepared.

**If not expected:** the patrol demands their business and expects them to announce their intentions. Make a peaceful gesture and state the business, or simply say you are "passing through". The patrol may let them pass — especially if their lords are friendly — or bring them in for questioning.

> [!danger] Refusing to answer
> ==Resistance creates hostilities.== If the knights refuse information, the GM may add a modifier to a **Suspicious** roll for the patrol; on a success the patrol **reads the refusal as a hostile act**. If the Player-knights are outnumbered they are **attacked**, with a squire or messenger galloping to the castle as combat is joined. The lord musters his household knights and hastens to the spot. ==**Fighting the local patrol makes enemies, and the lord need show no mercy to strangers.**==

## 2 · At the gate

A gate open to local traffic still has **guards checking identity and business**, especially of armed and mounted knights.

If the gate is closed: ride up, knock, **blow a horn, ring a bell, or shout** until the **porter** comes. ==Porter is a prestigious post — he decides who enters immediately and who waits.== A successful **Courtesy** roll helps an unexpected party; the porter may admit them at once, but is **more likely to send word to his lord** while the knights **wait outside the gate, however miserable the weather**.

Eventually a **castellan, steward or other official** welcomes them. **Hospitality must be extended by the lord's man and accepted by the visitors.**

## 3 · The courtyard and quarters

Grooms and stewards offer to take the steeds; **knights should order their own squires to see to the horses** with the grooms' help. Squires typically **remain in or near the stables until summoned the next day**, guarding horses and belongings — though a knight may use his squire as body servant and messenger. Food is brought to them and they **bed down in the hay**.

**Quarters** vary with Period and the lord's wealth: a furnished tower room shared between the knights, or **a pile of clean straw in the hall**. ==Whoever normally occupies that room or floor space **is moved out** to make room.== A separate **guest house**, where one exists, is reserved for important visitors — **a successful Courtesy roll can open its door even to the lowliest knight**.

**Washing:** ewers, buckets, soap and towels are brought. A leader of significant rank is offered **a bath**, and the other knights may use it after him — ==**the water is not usually changed**==.

## 4 · Being introduced

The knights are escorted to the reigning lord or lady, who may receive them in the high seat, **in their bed chamber**, or while otherwise occupied.

==**The Player-knight with the highest Glory should reply for everyone** and make a **Courtesy** roll, which sets the tone of the encounter.== The lord may ask their business or destination but is **not bound to** — the **old Cymric custom is to offer hospitality first**.

Once welcomed, the knights are **at the mercy of their host** and are usually invited to stay. The lord is **under no obligation to see them again**; a successful **Courtesy** roll may earn an invitation **to dine with the lord's family**, for which the knights are expected to wear **their absolute best clothing** — and impressive manners or a generous host may see them **loaned costly robes for the stay**.

## 5 · During the stay

**Limited leave of the castle**, always with a court servant as escort who **deftly steers them away from what the lord does not want seen**. If that servant is a commoner, a successful **Folklore** roll puts them at ease, and a successful **Intrigue** roll may learn something of the castle's goings-on.

**Dining.** Seats appropriate to station, **bumping the residents down** to make room; notable or renowned knights may be offered the **high table**, possibly beside the lord. Dinner talk is boasting, good humour and trivialities — run the **Feasting** procedure if desired. A lord holding to the **old Cymric tradition** asks their business **only after the final course**, often preceded or followed by a **request for news of the outside world** — an opportunity for **Orate** rolls.

**Entertainment.** The lord may be showing off, may enjoy their company, or ==may be offering distractions to **delay a purposeful meeting** for political reasons==. Evenings bring musicians, jugglers, pantomimists and fools; wealthy courts add poetry and a renowned bard. Many lords keep the ancient tradition of **"passing the harp"** at the end of a meal. Knights may offer, or be commanded, to perform — **Compose, Orate, Play (Instrument), Singing**. A **dance** allows **Dancing** rolls, endearing them to the court and to anyone they are Flirting with. **Games of chance** use **Gaming**; drinking contests are a perennial favourite. **Caroling** may carry an implicit wager — the least entertaining member, by consensus vote, **suffers a forfeit**, often specified in the carol.

## 6 · Departing — begging one's leave

==**Once given Hospitality, knights must not depart without the lord's permission.**== This is **begging one's leave**, and it is a vital part of Hospitality. ==**Departing without it costs a point of Hospitality.**==

When the time is right, the knights may be given **food for the road, directions, and a guide** for the next leg, and offered a **parting stirrup cup of wine**. If they are held in esteem, they may be **asked to bear messages or tidings to other courts**.

## Diplomacy at Court

The simple version: meet **privately** with the lord or lady — and their councillors, clerks and priests — and state your case.

- The opposing diplomat may be the lord, the lady, or a representative. ==**Determine the opposing diplomat's Courtesy with 3D6+4**== — and they may well be famous for it.
- **An opposed Courtesy contest decides who gets their desires fulfilled.**
- It may be handled with **a single Courtesy roll**, or **several across days** of negotiation.
- For particularly difficult cases the GM may impose a **reflexive −5/+5, or even −10/+10**.
- **Gifts grease the wheels** — money handed out in "gifts" is a normal part of the work.

> **Worked example (Clydno the Diplomat).** Sir Clydno negotiates with a neighbouring baron for (1) free access along the trade roads, (2) relinquishment of hunting rights in Harewood, (3) tollage rights at Highwater Bridge. His lord gives him **£10 in Roman silver**; he hands out **£4 in gifts** and makes **three opposed Diplomacy rolls** against courtiers and the baron. He **wins two** — gaining the roads and the hunting rights — but the baron **will not budge on tollage**.

## Spying at Court

To spy is to seek information from someone who wants it kept private — **a treacherous undertaking**.

- It is **hard to find time alone with anyone but the assigned escort**, who may be probed — but who ==may also have been **educated to resist Intrigue** with his own Intrigue Skill==.
- First **establish rapport** to persuade someone to talk: **Flirting**, seduction, a Skill demonstrating shared interest (**Falconry, Hunting, Heraldry, Gaming**), **giving a gift** (valued in £), **doing a Favor**, hunting or hawking together, a short adventure together, and so on.
- Then an **opposed Intrigue** resolution between the Player-knight and the informant decides the outcome — the informant **knows not to share certain things with outsiders**.
