A potentially dangerous act, **whatever the stations of those involved** — discretion is advised.

## Seducing a commoner

A successful **Flirting** roll, which triggers an **opposed Chaste/Lustful** roll.

The GM may apply **negative modifiers to Flirting** — the commoner is happily married or in love with someone else, the Player-knight has **low APP**, or has previously failed important Courtly Skill rolls such as Dancing. Conversely, **high APP or significant Glory** may earn a **bonus**.

## Seducing a noblewoman or courtier

Social equals, raised in an atmosphere that **continually warns against smooth-talking, insincere visitors**. A **Christian** court is likely to emphasize and honor chastity; even in a **Pagan** court, public wantonness may be frowned upon depending on the time of year. **It requires much effort.**

- ==**The Player-knight must first perform a Favor** for them, of a value depending on their rank and status== — valuable gifts, deeds, or dangerous actions.
- ==The GM **secretly sets a minimum threshold** for how many Favors will convince them.==
- **Secrecy is absolutely required** for the safety and reputation of everyone involved.

## Consequences and discovery

**If a liaison between a man and a woman is discovered**, the GM is free to **roll for childbirth in the Winter Phase** to see whether the union was productive.

**With a common servant**, depending on the disposition of the lord or lady, **the servant may be beaten or lose their position**. The Player-knight's reputation usually suffers only slightly — but **greatly** if the court particularly upholds Christian morality, or if the servant was the lord or lady's personal favorite, whatever their religion. ==**Honor loss is ensured if the seduction appears in any way non-consensual.**==

## Adultery

**Blatantly dangerous and illegal.** Adultery is sex with someone outside a marriage — a **legally binding contract** tied to property and inheritance rights. Canon Law does not discriminate between men and women, but **custom weighs heavily in favor of the husband**: a man whose wife has committed adultery is **cuckolded**, and lives in fear that any child she bears in the next year is not of his blood.

Being cuckolded is a **serious dishonor requiring revenge**:

- Catching wife and lover **in flagrante delicto**, the husband has the ==**legal right to kill the cuckolder**==. If **Merciful**, he may merely **castrate** him.
- **Either act restores the Honor lost** to the cuckolding.
- He may **send his wife to a nunnery**, or keep her to perform her duties as steward — but little more.

> **This is the state of things at the start of the campaign.** As the Arthurian ethos evolves, many nobles develop a more forgiving understanding of seduction and being seduced.
