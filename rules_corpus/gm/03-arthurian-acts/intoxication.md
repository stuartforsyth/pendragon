Alcohol flows freely in Arthur's Britain. **Ale** is the commonest — village alewives brew it from malted barley with herbs like clover and heather, and sell it from their dwellings (*alehouses*). **Beer** is ale flavoured with hops. **Mead** is fermented honey, spices and herbs, preferred among the less Romanized Cymric nobility. **Wine** is preferred in the cities and among the Roman clergy, imported in quantity from the continent. **Hard liquor has not yet been discovered.**

Common ale is **"small ale"** — watery and weak, and hard to get drunk on. Wealthier lords keep professional brew masters making stronger ales spiced with imported cinnamon and ginger, and serve copious mead and **unmixed** wine. This accounts for much of the outrageous behaviour of guests used to weaker brew, who get roaring drunk on an equal volume of the stronger stuff.

Severity is set by an **Indulgent** roll; the state is then **maintained by an Energetic roll** once every two hours.

## Table 3.11 — Intoxication Results (unopposed **Indulgent**)

| Indulgent result | State of Intoxication | Modifiers |
|---|---|---|
| **Critical** | **Roaring Drunk** | **+10** to Lustful, Reckless, Indulgent and Valorous; **−10 to all Skills** |
| **Success** | **Drunk** | **+5** to Flirting, Orate, Lustful, Reckless, Indulgent and Valorous; **−5** to all other Skills |
| **Failure** | Sober | No modifiers |
| **Fumble** | Sober | **No Indulgent rolls need be made for the remainder of the scene** |

**A critical is the worst outcome on this table**, and a fumble removes the character from the drinking entirely.

**Modifiers to Indulgent:**

- **Mead:** +2
- **Wine:** +5 (or higher for exotic wines)
- **Feasting in the presence of enemies:** −5
- **Attending a royal feast:** −2

**No experience checks while drunk.** A character who is intoxicated has impaired awareness of their own actions and so cannot learn from them — **they gain no experience checks to their Skills.**

## Table 3.12 — Intoxicated Activity Level (unopposed **Energetic**)

Intoxication lasts **as long as the drinker is awake**. Roll **Energetic for every two hours of game time — equal to one Feast Round** — until everyone retires.

| Energetic result | State | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| **Critical** | **Sobered up** | No modifiers; the character's state is now **Sober** |
| **Success** | Dull and sleepy | **−5** to all Skills |
| **Failure** | **Passed out** | No activity; if woken, **−10** to all Skills |
| **Fumble** | **Comatose** | **Will not awake under any circumstances until the next morning** |

**Always keep the worst result of the previous rolls**, and the penalties from this roll are **added to** any arising from the character's intoxication level.

**Modifiers to Energetic:**

- **Roaring Drunk:** −10
- **Drunk:** −5
- **Debilitated:** −5

## The hangover

Characters who **lose consciousness** must roll **CON the next day at −5** (**−10 if comatose**). **Failure indicates a hangover: −5 to all Skills for the remainder of the day.**

## Where this is invoked

The **drink cards** in the Feast Event deck ("More Drink", "Mead", "Raucous Contest", "Tipsy", "Overindulgence", and the rest) all defer to these rules — see [[gm.arthurian-acts.feasting]]. Note that **Temperate/Indulgent** decisions on those cards come first; this table resolves what the drinking then does.
