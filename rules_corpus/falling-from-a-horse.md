If you are unseated, thrown, or falling from a horse, the primary roll you make to minimize or avoid damage is an **unopposed Horsemanship roll**. However, depending on the exact circumstances of your fall, the rules also call for an **unopposed DEX roll** in certain situations.

Here is how both rolls work to keep your knight safe:

### **1. The Horsemanship Roll (The Standard Fall)**

When you are about to fall, be thrown from your mount, or if your horse slips or falls due to wounds, you attempt an **Emergency Dismount** using your **Horsemanship skill**. The outcomes are resolved as follows:

- **Critical Success:** You land gracefully on your feet and begin the next round upright (if conscious). You take **no damage** from the fall and retain all of your weapons.
- **Normal Success:** You land on your feet upright and ready to act next round. You take **no damage** from the fall, but you drop any weapons you were holding.
- **Failure:** You fail to dismount properly. You take **1D6 falling damage** and drop all held weapons.
- **Fumble:** You take **2D6 falling damage** and drop your weapons. Additionally, if your horse fell as well, it lands directly on top of you, inflicting its full normal Damage value (though your armor protects against the horse's weight normally).

Alternatively, if you are falling from a horse in a more general sense, a successful Horsemanship roll simply **reduces the falling damage dice by 1D6** (or by **2D6 on a critical success**). Be careful, though: a fumble on this roll **doubles** the number of damage dice you take!

---

### **2. The DEX Roll (Unconsciousness and General Fall-Breaking)**

There are specific exceptions where the rules direct you to use your **DEX characteristic** instead of Horsemanship to survive a fall:

- **Falling Unconscious:** If you suffer a Major Wound or your Hit Points drop below your Unconscious threshold while mounted, you will begin to slide off your horse. In this life-or-death moment, you attempt an Emergency Dismount using an **unopposed DEX roll**. A success means you fall gently and take **no damage**; a failure means you suffer a bad fall for **1D6 damage**.
- **Breaking a Fall:** The Gamemaster’s Handbook notes that the hazard of being thrown or knocked from a horse, or falling from a height, can be offset with a **DEX roll**.
- **Other Falls:** If you are falling from anything _other_ than a horse (like a castle wall or a steep slope), a **DEX roll** is used instead of Horsemanship to reduce the damage dice.
