Captured knights buy freedom with a **ransom** paid to the captor — a key reason knights
spare each other. Lords ransom household knights; mercenaries supply their own (or their
families); landed knights collect from their vassals. **Rule of thumb: ransom = the
person's normal income/maintenance.**

- **Surrender oath:** a surrendering knight swears **not to escape** (Honor binds them).
- **Nobles** always become the **king's property**; Arthur pays about **half** the value.
- **Sharing:** captors divide the ransom as they agree (helping GM characters get a
  share).
- **Collecting:** prisoners stay captive until paid — chained, free-roaming, or released
  on their **word of honor** to return with funds (or back to captivity within **a year
  and a day**). A **ransom broker** pays **50%** at once and takes over the claim.
- **Tribal cultures** recognize ransom, but hatred often means the captive is killed or
  enslaved instead.

## Ransom values

**Table 12.1 — Knights:** Squire £1 · Mercenary Esquire £2 · Mercenary Knight £3 ·
Household Knight £4 · Vassal (Poor Manor) £8 · Vassal (Normal) £10 · Vassal (Multiple
Manors) £20+ · Banneret £100.

**Table 12.2 — Nobles:** Baron £300 · Rich Baron £600 · Count/Duke/Petty King £1,000 ·
King/independent ruler £3,000.

**Table 12.3 — Soldiers:** Spearman/Swordsman/Archer 60d. · Guardsman 120d. · Leader of
10 120d. · Leader of 100 £1 · Cavalryman £2 · Leader of 1,000 £4.

**Table 12.4 — Clergy:** Lay Member 120d. · Priest/Monk/Canon £1 · Bishop/Abbot £300 ·
Archbishop £2,000.

**Table 12.5 — Tribal:** *Saxons* — Ceorl none, Heorthgeneat £2, Thegn £4, Ætheling £40,
King £400. *Picts* — Warrior none, Chieftain £2. *Irish* — Kern none, Bonnacht £2,
Chieftain £20.
