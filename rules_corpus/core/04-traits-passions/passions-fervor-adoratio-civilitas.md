## Fervor — Love & Hate

Opposites; you cannot hold **both** Love and Hate for the same subject (though
Hate (Cornishmen) + Love (Tristram the Cornishman) is fine). Their bonuses apply only
to matters concerning the subject.

- **Hate (Person/Group)** — invoked on mere **awareness of proximity** (no contact
  needed), making Exalted Hates dangerous in peaceful settings. Choose the subject
  carefully — too broad brings constant grief, too narrow is useless. Restraint =
  opposed Hate vs another Passion/Trait (Homage, Chivalry; or Forgiving/Just/Merciful/
  Trusting).
- **Love (Family)** — a **Universal Passion** every character has; invoked when family
  is endangered. (Family = immediate blood/adopted; Kin = extended; Lineage = common
  ancestor; Clan = close-knit related families.)
- **Love (Person)** — a tender/physical bond gained in play (GM may impose it). It is
  also the **successor to Adoration** once consummated, keeping the same value. Invoked
  when the subject is in danger.

## Adoratio — Adoration & Devotion

- **Adoration (Beloved)** — the chaste Passion of **fine amor / Romance**. The Beloved
  must be **apparently unattainable** and initially rejects the Lover; the Beloved's
  feelings don't matter — inspiration comes from their mere existence, and suffering
  deepens it. Two huge advantages: grants a **+10 Skill bonus regardless of
  Inspiration/Impassionment**, and may be invoked **once per day at any time for any
  reason**. May cause **Misery**; Famous/Exalted Adorers risk **Melancholy** at
  reminders of the Beloved. Requires complete chastity; **becomes Love if consummated**
  (moving to Fervor).
  - **Involuntary Adoration:** on first seeing someone of Transcendent Beauty/power
    (Ygraine, Guenever, Isoud, Lancelot, Tristram…), roll **Lustful, Arbitrary, and
    Honest** — if **all three succeed**, gain Adoration at **1D6** (+5 for Transcendent
    Beauty, +10 for exemplars like Guenever/Galahad). This is the only way to hold more
    than one Adoration at once.
- **Devotion (Deity)** — faith in a supernatural being; confers no knowledge or powers,
  and does **not** count as Directed. Acquired at will but **cannot inspire until the
  character qualifies as a Religious Knight** (p.127); then invoked at blasphemy, abuse
  of a holy place/person, or when championing the religion.

## Civilitas — Chivalry, Hospitality & Station

- **Chivalry** — the strong protect the weak; act honorably toward ladies, commoners,
  and knights. Generated by declaring the desire, but **cannot inspire until qualified
  as a Chivalrous knight**. May conflict with **Station** (resolve by opposed roll).
  Invoked when a chivalric virtue is offended or there's a chance to uphold the Code
  (defend a lady, protect a commoner). The Chivalric Code forbids e.g. missile weapons
  against knights and requires mercy (to deny a hated foe mercy needs a **failed
  Merciful roll**), generosity (sharing Glory with fellows), fairness, and courtesy.
- **Hospitality** — a Universal Passion; offer food/lodging/safety without expectation.
  Inside a hearth, **peace must reign** even between enemies, and a guest must help
  defend it; a guest may not leave without the host's permission, and the host may
  never act against a guest. Invoked by the wronged party when host or guest breaks
  Hospitality, or when a knight witnesses a violation. (Not invoked merely because your
  home is attacked from outside — but yes if an invited guest draws a weapon, or a
  permitted guest is attacked.)
- **Station** — a Universal Passion upholding the social order (king > nobles >
  knights > esquires > commoners). Invoked when witnessing offenses to station (a
  commoner failing deference, taking liberties, or offering violence to nobles; a
  protected commoner attacked by foreigners; a knight rebuked for abusing a commoner; a
  lower noble offending a higher). Knights **lose Honor** by behaving like commoners
  (manual labor, hawking goods, non-holding business). Chivalrous knights often make
  opposed rolls **against** their own Station.
