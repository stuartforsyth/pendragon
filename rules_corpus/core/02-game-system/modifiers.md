Modifiers are **temporary** bonuses (up) or penalties (down) to **Statistic
values**. They always apply to the **Statistic, never to the die roll** — the only
thing ever added to a roll is the **critical bonus** (see
[[core.game-system.critical-bonus]]). The GM may grant or deny them at discretion.

## Values less than 1

A value reduced to **0 or below** counts simply as **0** (negative Statistics are
not used, except Hit Points in Ch.11). You still roll, and **failure is
automatic** — but the **fumble range widens by one point for every point below
zero**. E.g. Dancing 8 with a −10 modifier counts as 0 (not −2); its fumble range
becomes **18–20**.

## Table 2.1 — Skill Modifiers

| Nature of challenge | Modifier | When to use |
|--------------------|----------|-------------|
| Ordinary | +5 or −5 | Most situations |
| Strong | +10 or −10 | Dramatic situations |
| Extreme | +15 or −15 | Heroic situations |

Extreme is normally the largest, but **±20 (called Epic)** is possible on rare
occasions.

## Types of modifier

- **Trait & Passion modifiers** — applied when circumstances affect behavior/
  emotional state. All Trait modifiers are **reflexive**: a −10 Temperate is the
  same as +10 Indulgent. Players may request one when circumstances warrant; the
  recommended size is **+5 or −5**.
- **Characteristic modifiers** — usually penalties from external restriction (e.g.
  DEX −5 to sneak in a mail hauberk).
- **Damage modifiers** — add/subtract **dice** from damage: e.g. Weapon-Skill
  critical **+4D6**, unarmed Brawling critical **+2D6**, two-handed weapons
  **+2D6**, a bow **−1D6**. Some weapons instead modify the total rolled.
- **Skill modifiers** — make an action harder/easier (most combat modifiers are
  Skill modifiers based on weapon type, position, etc.). Use Table 2.1 for
  guidance.

## Reflexive modifiers (+X / −Y)

A reflexive modifier affects **both** values in an opposed resolution — a bonus to
one and a penalty to the other — written like **+5/−5** (a mounted knight gets +5
attacking a footman, who gets −5). Reflexive and other modifiers stack and may
cancel.

**A +5/−5 reflexive modifier is *not* the same as a single +10.** Example: Sword 15
vs 1H-Hafted 15 —
- With **+5/−5** → Sword 20 vs 1H-Hafted 10: the knight criticals 5% of the time,
  the knave succeeds (incl. partial) half the time.
- With a single **+10** → Sword 20 (+5) vs 1H-Hafted 15: the knight criticals 30%
  of the time, the knave succeeds three-quarters of the time.
