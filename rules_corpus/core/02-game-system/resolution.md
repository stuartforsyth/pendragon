A single **roll-under d20 mechanic** resolves every action. Most things a
character does succeed automatically and need no roll — you roll only when an
action requires skill/discipline under stress, scrutiny, or haste, or when an
outside force acts on the character.

## Unopposed resolution — four outcomes

Roll 1D20 directly against the pertinent Statistic value:

- **Critical success** — roll **exactly equals** the (modified) value. The
  ultimate success, with extra benefits; always warrants a **check** to a Trait,
  Passion, or Skill, and sometimes grants extra Glory. Always compare against the
  *final modified* value (Skill 6 with +5 → 11: an 11 now criticals, a 6 is just a
  success).
- **Success** — roll **less than** the value. If significant, the GM *may* grant a
  check (not automatic just because a roll was made).
- **Failure** — roll **greater than** the value. The action fails / the Trait or
  Passion is not activated.
- **Fumble** — a **natural 20**, *unless* the value is 20 (then 20 is a critical
  and no fumble is possible). A disastrous stroke of bad luck (drop/break a weapon,
  cough mid-ballad). GM's option: a fumble may call for a check — we learn from our
  worst mistakes. A fumble is not a licence for the GM to inflict endless grief.

**Characteristics** are pass/fail only: no critical/fumble distinction and no
checks.

## Opposed resolution — five outcomes

Made like an unopposed roll but compared against another roll (an opponent's
Statistic or a fixed value). To **win** you must both succeed *and* roll higher
than the opponent.

- **Win** — succeed (roll ≤ value) and roll higher than the opponent. A **critical
  beats any non-critical** and guarantees at least a tie — even a value-1 character
  rolling a 1 wins, since all criticals are equal.
- **Loss** — roll greater than your value (both parties can lose). A **fumbled**
  loss adds insult to injury (in combat, you drop/break your weapon).
- **Partial success** — you succeed (roll < value) but roll **lower** than the
  opponent. Usually a minor benefit (in combat, you still get your Shield/Parry
  Protection).
- **Tie** — both succeed and roll the **same** number. Critical values are never
  counted for size, so if **both critical, it is a tie**. In combat a tie means
  both strike (with exceptions — see Ch.7).
- **Mutual failure** — both fail; nothing happens, situation unresolved.

### Friendly opposed resolution

When several players roll the same Skill/Trait, the **highest successful** result
wins the contest; only that winner receives a check.

## Fixed opposition

Against passive resistance (break a door, resist a poison), roll opposed against a
**fixed value**. The roll must succeed *and* beat that value — so a character whose
Statistic is ≤ the fixed value can only win on a **critical**. (A door of value 15
needs a STR roll that succeeds and beats 15.)
