Skills fall into one or more of three **uses**. Reserve rolls for when success/failure
makes a meaningful difference.

## Deeds

Doing something specific, public, and important — make the relevant Skill/Combat Skill
roll (opposed or unopposed).

- **Critical:** action done, take a check; GM may grant a **+ modifier** to a follow-up
  Trait/Passion/Skill roll.
- **Success:** action done; check only if significant.
- **Failure:** action not accomplished.
- **Fumble:** not accomplished, but take a check; GM may impose a **− modifier** to a
  follow-up roll.

## Inquiry

Accessing the body of knowledge a Skill implies (Sword → judge a blade's quality;
Horsemanship → a horse's breed; Courtesy → what to do at a coronation). Usually an
**unopposed** roll.

- **Critical:** knows a great deal (+check, + follow-up modifier).
- **Success:** knows enough (check if significant).
- **Failure:** can't recall the information.
- **Fumble:** doesn't know, but takes a check; GM may impose − modifiers — and may use
  **Secret Rolls** to conceal the fumble and feed false information.

## Parlance

Using a Skill to communicate/put others at ease, tailored to the audience: **Courtesy**
with nobles, **Religion** with clergy, **Folklore** with commoners; **Orate** and
(deceptively) **Intrigue** for persuasion. A Courtesy/Religion/Folklore roll may be
made **before** an Orate/Intrigue roll to enhance it. Results mirror the above
(critical/success/failure/fumble → communicated / not).

## Using a leader's Skills

Skills like **Battle** and **Hunting** may have a **leader** roll on behalf of a group
(the followers must agree to follow, or rank decides). Only the leader rolls; followers
get **no check**.

## Improving Skills

Skills increase permanently during the **Winter Phase** (field experience, training,
practice, reputation, or supernatural means). See Ch.14.
