Honor is a noble's integrity/worth and the public trust and recognition granted to
them. It is inborn to nobles (not needed by churchmen or commoners), upholds social
norms (respect superiors, uphold a lord's name, loyalty, hospitality, class rules),
and includes a personal code that **demands a response if questioned**. It **cannot be
seized/bought/traded — only granted** by others recognizing it. **Glory without Honor
is the mark of an unworthy wretch.**

- **Ranges (1–20):** Unsung 1–4, Normal 5–15, Famous 16–20, Exalted 20+x.
- **Below 5 Honor** → lose title, cast out of noble society. **Honor 0** → disgraced
  forever, removed from play (can never be raised again).
- May be used **exactly like a Passion** to gain a bonus to one Skill when defending
  Honor (see [[core.traits-passions.passion-roll-inspiration-and-crisis]]).

## Public vs. private Honor

Honor measures what the **public** knows. Secret dishonorable acts are noted as a
parenthetical negative, e.g. `Honor 14 (−3)`. **All Honor rolls use the true value**
(modified by hidden dishonor); if the acts are exposed, the parenthetical is
permanently subtracted. The GM may rule that a private honor-gaining/losing deed causes
**no change until exposed**.

## Gaining Honor

Via experience checks — an honorable deed **beyond normal duty**, usually per a
Trait/Passion. Examples (with the enabling Trait):

- Clemency to a foe who didn't ask (Forgiving); alms ≥£10 / like donation / ≥1/10 of a
  non-relative's ransom (Generous); keeping a secret under torture (Honest); a lawful
  decision against self-interest (Just); mercy to a knight who didn't ask (Merciful);
  sharing fresh Glory with fellows (Modest); upholding religion under adversity
  (Spiritual); valorous battle feats — re-entering under half HP, fighting on when
  others flee, taking up a fallen standard, surviving 3:1 odds, winning the battle
  (Valorous); keeping the peace under Hospitality; defending a liege / obeying against
  self-preservation (Homage); obeying an unwise order (Fealty/Loyalty); holding order
  vs a mob (Station).

## Defending Honor

When someone **publicly** questions/insults your Honor or a deeply-held virtue, you
**must act or lose Honor** (a Chivalrous knight whose valor is doubted; a Religious
knight whose faith is mocked). You must also defend the Honor of family, liege, and
king.

## Oaths

An oath is a promise under divine witness — the most sacred bond; breakers are shunned.
Make an appropriate **Passion roll** when taking an important oath; Honor at stake:
**Critical 3, Success 2, Failure 1, Fumble 0.** A PK may have only **one active oath**
at a time.

- **Positive oath** (vow to accomplish): gain the staked Honor on success; **lose** it
  on failure.
- **Negative oath** (vow never to do): **immediately gain** the staked Honor, but
  **lose double** if ever broken.
