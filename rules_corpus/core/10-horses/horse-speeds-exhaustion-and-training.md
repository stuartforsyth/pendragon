## Gaits

- **Walk** — slowest; sets the Movement Rate.
- **Trot** — Move ×2 · **Canter** — Move ×3 · **Gallop** — Move ×6.

Any gait holds for a scene, but **galloping over 15 minutes risks the horse's health**.
**Ambling** is a special smooth gait (same pace as a trot, far less tiring) only certain
individual horses have; they cost much more.

## Riding to death (overuse)

Overuse comes from galloping >15 min, riding day and night, no rest, a forced march, or
poor food/water. At the end of the abuse, roll the horse's **CON** and consult Table
10.1.

### Table 10.1 — Horse Exhaustion Roll

| Horse CON roll | Effect |
|----------------|--------|
| Critical | No problem |
| Success | Too tired to continue; recovers with food and rest |
| Failure | **Blown** — cannot canter/gallop and never fully recovers |
| Fumble | Horse **drops dead** |

## Training

Light training at **age 3**, proper training at **4**: ~**1 year** for basic riding,
**2 years** to be trusted for mounted combat. War horses peak around **8**, decline
after ~**15**, and retire from heavy work by **20**. Best trained by being ridden by a
skilled knight through many experiences.

**Breeding** determines potential — destriers come only from destriers, amblers from
amblers (with some fluidity: a great courser might make a decent charger). All horses
know the simple trick of **standing in place** when the rider dismounts or is unhorsed.
**Unique tricks** (come when called, rear, dance, count, etc.) cost **+25% (one), +50%
(two), +75% (three features)**.
